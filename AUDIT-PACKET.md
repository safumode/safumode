## 2. Authority, key-loss, and pause matrix

There is no owner, connected-wallet administrator, proxy admin, upgrade key, or privileged relayer. Any account may deposit, relay signed calls, activate matured operations, and execute matured withdrawals. Privilege resides in two EIP-712 signing keys and one immutable destination:

- **A — `authorizer`:** signs trusted-recipient immediate withdrawals; signs delayed withdrawals; unilaterally revokes trust; can cancel withdrawals; can pause; co-signs trust additions, resume, and rotation.
- **R — `recoveryAuthorizer`:** immediately recovers only to immutable `recoveryRecipient`; can cancel withdrawals; can pause; co-signs trust additions, resume, and rotation.
- **D — `recoveryRecipient`:** immutable recipient of `recover`; it has no signing privilege merely by being D.

| Condition | Immediate trusted withdrawal | Schedule/execute delayed withdrawal | Recover to D | Revoke trust | Add/activate trust | Cancel withdrawal / rotation | Rotate authorities | Pause / resume |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal, A+R available | A | A / permissionless after delay | R | A | A+R / permissionless after delay | either authority | A+R / permissionless after delay | either may pause / A+R resume |
| A lost, unpaused | unavailable | unavailable to schedule; an existing current-epoch request remains permissionlessly executable unless cancelled | R still works to D | unavailable | unavailable | R can cancel | unavailable | R may pause; cannot resume alone |
| R lost, unpaused | A | A / permissionless after delay | unavailable | A | unavailable | A can cancel | unavailable | A may pause; cannot resume alone |
| Both lost, unpaused | unavailable | only already-scheduled current-epoch requests can execute | unavailable | unavailable | pending activations remain permissionless if otherwise valid | unavailable | unavailable | unavailable |
| Paused, both available | blocked | scheduling and execution blocked | blocked | **still allowed by A** | scheduling and activation blocked | withdrawal cancellation and rotation cancellation remain allowed | scheduling and activation blocked | A+R can resume |
| Paused with A or R lost | blocked | blocked | blocked | only possible if A survives | blocked | surviving authority can perform its cancellation powers | blocked | **permanently stuck paused absent recovery of the lost key** |

The final row is deliberate and severe: pausing is unilateral, but unpausing is 2-of-2. Rotation is also blocked while paused and itself requires both current keys. The contract contains no escape hatch for a lost key. `test/AuthorizationVault.key-loss.js` directly exercises the paused single-key-loss lock and the asymmetric unpaused capabilities.

## 3. ERC20 exact-outbound limitation

For every ERC20 outflow (`withdrawTrusted`, `executeWithdrawal`, and `recover`), `_transfer` samples both vault and recipient `balanceOf` values and requires the vault debit and recipient credit to each equal the signed `amount`. A mismatch reverts with `ExactTransferMismatch`, rolling back token movement and the associated nonce/executed-state mutation.

Consequences:

- fee-on-transfer, sender-fee, rebasing/reflection, or other non-exact balance semantics are intentionally unsupported;
- a positive ERC20 transfer from the vault to itself cannot satisfy both exact deltas and reverts;
- zero transfers pass only when both observed balances are unchanged;
- native-asset transfers use a low-level value call and are not subject to ERC20 balance-delta accounting;
- deposits are different: `depositToken` records the actual vault balance increase and therefore may accept less than the requested amount, but this does **not** make that token withdrawable if its outbound behavior is non-exact;
- token contracts can still deny service by reverting, returning malformed values, failing `balanceOf`, or changing behavior. Compatibility is not an allowlist or guarantee.

The focused evidence is `test/AuthorizationVault.exact-outbound.js`; adversarial token fixtures are in `contracts/MockToken.sol`.

## 4. Threat model and invariants to challenge

### Assumed threats

Review against arbitrary relayers/callers, signature replay or cross-domain reuse, compromised A or R (separately), malicious recipients, malicious/non-standard ERC20s, callback reentrancy, front-running of signed messages and permissionless activations, stale queued state across rotation, timestamp/deadline boundaries, RPC omission/equivocation/reorgs in tooling, and corrupted/replayed local monitor files.

Not solved by the contract: compromise of both authorities; compromise of R together with D; permanent key loss; malicious or unavailable RPCs; host/filesystem compromise; downstream alert delivery; social/operational failures; or unsupported token economics.

### Core invariants

1. No outflow occurs without a valid current-domain authority signature and the applicable destination/delay policy, except permissionless execution of a previously valid matured request.
2. `recover` has no caller-selected destination and always targets immutable D.
3. A alone cannot recover; R alone cannot authorize normal withdrawals; neither alone can add trust, resume, or rotate.
4. Either authority can stop outflows; paused state blocks all outflows and schedule/activation paths listed above; resume is strictly A+R.
5. Untrusted destinations cannot use the immediate path; trusted destinations cannot be queued through the delayed path.
6. Trust addition and rotation are 2-of-2 and delayed. Trust revocation is immediate by A. Either authority may veto a pending rotation.
7. Nonces/deadlines prevent replay as intended, failed calls roll back nonce consumption, and EIP-712 binds name, epoch-specific version, chain ID, verifying contract, type, and fields.
8. Rotation retires old keys, increments `authorityEpoch`, changes `VERSION()`, invalidates prior-epoch trust and queued withdrawals/additions, and prevents reinstalling retired/current authorities.
9. External token/native calls cannot reenter protected outflow/deposit functions or produce partial state changes after revert.
10. Every successful ERC20 outflow has exact signed vault-debit and recipient-credit deltas.
11. Constructor and delayed timestamps cannot overflow the contract's `uint64` execution fields.
12. Cancellation remains available while paused where the source permits it and cannot revive or execute cancelled/stale operations.

## 6. Manifest, UI, and monitor limitations

### Manifest

The manifest schema is explicitly `safumode-local-deployment-v1` and verification insists on Hardhat chain ID 31337. It pins runtime code hash, constructor/deployment evidence, compiler input/build identity, source hashes, bytecode hashes, and lockfile hash. Verification depends on the configured RPC returning code, state, creation transaction, and receipt, and (unless skipped in library use) on the local build. It is reproducibility evidence for a local deployment only—not public-chain provenance, an audit, an immutable registry, or protection from a malicious RPC. Mutable current authorities are not compared to constructor values; the creation transaction proves initial values. `web/deployment-allowlist.json` is not populated with an endorsed public deployment, and the UI does not consume the local manifest as a trust root.

### Monitor

`scripts/safumode-monitor.js` is read-only and verifies configured chain ID, address code hash, block/log/receipt consistency, retained reorg ancestry, and local checkpoint identity. It emits JSON lines to stdout and stores a local retry spool plus append-only delivered journal. It requires a trusted archival RPC with complete blocks, receipts, and logs. Important limits:

- stdout completion does not prove a downstream paging/notification system received an alert; the journal is only a local durable copy;
- it watches the finite event ABI embedded in the script, not arbitrary calls, storage changes, deposits, mempool intent, key compromise, or missing events outside that list;
- confirmation lag is configurable, so alerts are delayed and shallow reorg handling depends on retained depth;
- a reorg beyond retained history, inconsistent/omitting RPC, corrupt/oversized files, lock conflict, disk failure, or spool limits causes failure/operator intervention rather than guaranteed observation;
- code-hash matching authenticates bytes only relative to the configured expected hash and RPC; it does not establish that the expected contract is safe;
- host/filesystem compromise and malicious-but-internally-consistent RPC responses remain outside its guarantees;
- no service supervisor, redundancy, retention policy, alert routing, or incident-response process is supplied.

The browser likewise labels RPC-derived state as unverified, authenticates neither the RPC nor public deployment identity, and cannot sign or submit transactions.

## 7. Questions for an external reviewer

1. Can any EIP-712 payload be replayed across functions, epochs, chains, deployments, nonce namespaces, or authority rotations, including after failed calls?
2. Are the asymmetric pause semantics complete and safe, especially `revokeTrust` and cancellation while paused, and is permanent paused key-loss acceptable?
3. Can pending trust, withdrawal, or rotation state survive/revive incorrectly through replacement, cancellation, pausing, or epoch changes?
4. Does any single-key compromise produce capabilities beyond the matrix, including through parameter confusion (`byRecovery`, `immediate`) or signature substitution?
5. Are 2-of-2 rotation, permanent retired-authority exclusion, and inability to recover a lost key appropriate, or do they create unacceptable availability/liveness risk?
6. Is exact outbound accounting safe for all claimed compatible ERC20s, including rebasing, hooks/callbacks, proxies that change behavior, unusual `balanceOf`, zero amounts, and self-transfer?
7. Are state updates correctly ordered around every external call, and is the bespoke reentrancy guard sufficient across native and token callbacks?
8. Are timestamp arithmetic, `uint64` conversion, deadline boundary (`>` rather than `>=`), sequential control/trust/rotation nonces, and arbitrary withdrawal nonces correct?
9. Can a malicious recipient or token force harmful denial of service, ambiguous accounting, or unexpected native-call behavior?
10. Does authority-epoch invalidation cover every capability and domain, and are old signatures/grants impossible to reactivate?
11. Do manifest fingerprinting and creation-transaction checks omit any material compiler/deployment input or admit RPC/local-artifact equivocation?
12. Does monitor reorg/checkpoint/spool/journal logic provide the documented at-least-replay behavior under crashes, torn writes, path replacement, RPC inconsistency, and repeated forks—and where can it miss or duplicate an alert?
13. Do tests encode contract behavior rather than desired policy, and which properties need independent fuzzing, invariant testing, symbolic execution, or formal specification?

## 8. Release blockers

Treat all of the following as blockers to any production or real-value release:

- independent professional contract and operational-tooling review is not completed and findings are not remediated/retested;
- no formalized production threat model, invariant suite, broader fuzz/formal-verification campaign, or public bug-bounty process;
- paused loss of either authority permanently freezes all outflows, while unpaused key loss also prevents rotation; no accepted recovery/liveness design or operational sign-off exists;
- no production key-generation, custody, quorum/separation, backup, disaster-recovery, signer-compromise, or immutable D governance procedure;
- token support policy/allowlist and adversarial compatibility qualification are absent; exact-outbound behavior excludes common token classes;
- no endorsed public deployment, reproducible public-chain deployment procedure, explorer/source verification, chain-specific configuration review, or trusted deployment registry/UI authentication;
- manifest tooling is local-only and the UI/RPC preview cannot prove identity;
- no production relayer/lifecycle service, redundant trusted RPC strategy, supervised monitor deployment, downstream durable alert delivery, runbooks, paging, retention, or incident drills;
- no defined response for malicious tokens/recipients, chain reorgs, RPC equivocation, monitor state corruption, disk exhaustion, or loss/compromise of A, R, D, or host;
- dependency/compiler provenance, CI release gates, reproducible clean-build evidence, and release artifact signing have not been established for a production release;
- the prototype's no-upgrade/no-admin design and permanent constructor choices (`delay`, D) have not received explicit deployment-specific risk acceptance.

Do not infer closure from passing tests. Any design change made in response to review requires renewed source/test review and regenerated deployment evidence.
