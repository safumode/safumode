THE EVERYDAY EXAMPLE

Imagine your browser wallet gets stolen.

The thief can take whatever is still in that wallet. But sending funds out of the vault needs a valid signature from its separate authorizer. A connected wallet or the person paying the gas fee cannot substitute for it.

If the authorizer signs a withdrawal to a trusted address, it can go through immediately. If the address is new, the request must sit through the vault’s configured delay. Either authority can cancel that queued request before execution—but somebody has to notice and act.

WHO CAN DO WHAT

01 / FUNDING

Daily wallet

Can deposit and pay to relay a valid instruction. Cannot authorize an exit just by being connected.

02 / ORDINARY EXIT

Authorizer

Signs the exact asset, amount, and recipient. New recipients wait; already trusted ones do not.

03 / EMERGENCY

Recovery authority

Can move funds immediately, but only to the recovery address fixed when the vault was created.

Changing who the two signers are requires both current authorities and the waiting period. Adding a trusted address also requires both and the delay. This is not “two signatures for every withdrawal”: the routes have different rules.

NEXT / KNOW THE LIMITS
